SECTION 1: POLICY STATEMENT, OBJECTIVES & REGULATORY SCOPE
================================================================================
It is the unconditional policy of Meridian Trust & Savings Bank ("MTSB" or "the Bank") to prohibit and actively prevent money laundering, terrorist financing, proliferation financing, sanctions evasion, tax evasion, and all predicate illicit financial activities.

This comprehensive Policy is established pursuant to the Bank Secrecy Act (31 U.S.C. § 5311 et seq.), as amended by Title III of the USA PATRIOT Act of 2001, the Anti-Money Laundering Act of 2020 (AMLA 2020), the Corporate Transparency Act (CTA), regulations issued by the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN, 31 CFR Chapter X), Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation rules (12 CFR Part 326, Subpart B), and the Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC, 31 CFR Part 500 et seq.).

The Bank's Board of Directors has designated a full-time, experienced Bank Secrecy Act Compliance Officer ("BSA Officer") vested with sufficient institutional authority, independence, and operational resources to administer the Bank's BSA/AML/OFAC Compliance Program.

SECTION 2: CUSTOMER IDENTIFICATION PROGRAM (CIP) - 31 CFR § 1020.220
================================================================================
2.1 Four Mandatory Elements of Customer Identification
Prior to opening any deposit, loan, trust, safe deposit box, or other account relationship, Bank personnel must collect, record, and verify four core pieces of identifying information for every individual customer:
1. Legal Name: Full first name, middle name (if applicable), and surname. Trade names or "doing business as" (DBA) monikers must be accompanied by the legal owner's individual identification.
2. Date of Birth: Verified to ensure legal contractual capacity (minimum age 18, or joint custody under Uniform Transfers to Minors Act [UTMA]).
3. Residential or Business Physical Address:
   - For individuals: Physical residential address. A P.O. Box is strictly impermissible as a primary address; a P.O. Box may only be accepted as an auxiliary mailing address when accompanied by verified physical residential coordinates.
   - For armed forces personnel: Army Post Office (APO) or Fleet Post Office (FPO) address.
   - For legal entities: Principal place of business, local operating branch, or registered agent physical address.
4. Identification Number:
   - U.S. Persons: Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), consisting of a Social Security Number (SSN) or Employer Identification Number (EIN).
   - Non-U.S. Persons: One or more of the following: Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN), passport number and country of issuance, alien identification card number, or number and country of issuance of another unexpired government-issued document evidencing nationality/residence and bearing a photograph.

2.2 Verification Standards: Documentary & Non-Documentary
Verification must occur within five (5) business days of account opening.
- Documentary Verification: Inspection of original, unexpired, government-issued photo credentials (e.g., State Driver's License, Real ID, U.S. or Foreign Passport, Military ID, Permanent Resident Card Form I-551). Document numbers, issuing authority, issue date, and expiration date must be recorded in the core banking system.
- Non-Documentary Verification: Automated corroboration through independent third-party consumer reporting repositories (LexisNexis Risk Solutions, ChexSystems, Experian, Early Warning Services), validating public records, telephone directory listings, utility records, and verifying matching SSN/DOB combinations.

2.3 Inability to Verify Identity
If customer identity cannot be verified within ten (10) business days, or if counterfeit/altered documentation is presented:
- The account will be subjected to an immediate administrative freeze prohibiting withdrawals, debit transactions, and outbound wire transfers.
- The BSA Department will review the file for potential Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) filing.
- The account will be formally closed and funds returned via cashier's check payable only to the source depositor, unless restricted by law enforcement or OFAC block instructions.

SECTION 3: CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE (CDD) & BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP - 31 CFR § 1010.230
================================================================================
3.1 Beneficial Ownership Rule for Legal Entity Customers
When opening an account for a legal entity (corporation, LLC, partnership, general partnership, business trust), MTSB must obtain a completed, certified FinCEN Beneficial Ownership Certification Form identifying:
1. The Ownership Prong: Each individual who directly or indirectly owns 25 percent (25%) or more of the equity interests of the legal entity customer. (For high-risk business categories, MTSB internal policy lowers this threshold to 10%).
2. The Control Prong: One single individual with significant responsibility to control, manage, or direct the legal entity customer (e.g., Chief Executive Officer, Chief Financial Officer, Managing Member, General Partner, or President).

For every beneficial owner and controlling individual identified, full CIP (Name, DOB, Address, SSN/Passport) must be collected and verified prior to account activation.

3.2 Customer Risk Profiling & Tiers
Every customer account is assigned an automated Anti-Money Laundering risk rating upon onboarding, dynamically updated via behavioral monitoring:
- Low Risk: W-2 salaried employees, established local small businesses with predictable domestic cash flows, municipal entities, school districts.
- Medium Risk: Operating commercial enterprises with moderate wire volumes, multi-state trade operations, regional retail merchandisers.
- High Risk: Requiring mandatory Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD):
  * Cash-Intensive Businesses (convenience stores, liquor stores, laundromats, vending machine operators, privately owned ATMs).
  * Non-Bank Financial Institutions & Money Services Businesses (MSBs), currency exchangers, check cashers, check-cashing agents.
  * Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASPs), cryptocurrency exchanges, decentralized finance entities.
  * Non-Resident Aliens (NRAs) and foreign commercial entities.
  * Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs): Senior foreign or domestic political figures, immediate family members, and close associates.
  * Commercial real estate holding companies with complex multi-tiered offshore structures.

3.3 Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) Protocols
High-risk accounts undergo:
- Verification of source of wealth and source of funds (tax returns, audited financials, brokerage statements).
- Identification of primary suppliers, customers, and expected monthly volume of cash and international wire transfers.
- Mandatory site visits and physical facility inspections for commercial entities.
- Annual or semi-annual formal EDD account re-certification by the BSA Compliance Department.

SECTION 4: SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITY REPORTING (SAR) - 31 CFR § 1020.320
================================================================================
4.1 Mandatory SAR Filing Thresholds
MTSB must file a Suspicious Activity Report (FinCEN Form 111) with FinCEN regarding any transaction (or series of aggregate transactions) conducted or attempted by, at, or through the Bank, involving $5,000 or more ($25,000 if no suspect is identified), when the Bank knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect that:
1. Illicit Source: The transaction involves funds derived from illegal activities or is intended to hide or disguise assets derived from illegal activities.
2. Structuring / Evasion: The transaction is designed to evade BSA reporting requirements, specifically Currency Transaction Reports (CTR) under 31 CFR § 1010.311 (e.g., conducting multiple cash deposits between $8,000 and $9,950 across branches or consecutive days).
3. No Business or Apparent Lawful Purpose: The transaction has no business or apparent lawful purpose, or is not the sort in which the particular customer would normally be expected to engage, and the Bank knows of no reasonable explanation after examining the facts.
4. Criminal Instrumentality: The transaction involves use of the Bank to facilitate criminal activity, including computer hacking, identity theft, ransomware payments, or wire fraud.
5. Insider Abuse: Any detected or suspected criminal violation involving any employee, officer, director, or agent of MTSB, regardless of the dollar amount.

4.2 Filing Deadlines & Governance
- Standard Filing: Within thirty (30) calendar days after the date of initial detection of facts that constitute a basis for filing.
- Delayed Identification: If no suspect is identified on the date of initial detection, the Bank may delay filing for an additional thirty (30) calendar days to attempt to identify a suspect, but in no case may filing exceed sixty (60) calendar days after initial detection.
- Ongoing Suspicious Activity: For ongoing activity, SAR updates must be filed every ninety (90) calendar days.
- SAR Committee: All draft SARs are reviewed and approved by the MTSB Executive SAR Committee, comprising the BSA Officer, Chief Legal Counsel, and Chief Risk Officer. Monthly summary reports of SAR filings are submitted to the Board of Directors Audit & Risk Committee.

4.3 Strict Prohibition Against SAR Disclosure ("Tipping Off") - 31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(2)
Federal law strictly prohibits any director, officer, employee, or agent of MTSB from notifying any person involved in the transaction, or any third party, that a transaction has been reported or that a SAR has been drafted or filed.
Unauthorized disclosure of a SAR or confidential SAR information is a federal felony carrying criminal penalties of up to five (5) years imprisonment and fines up to $250,000, in addition to civil monetary penalties.

SECTION 5: CURRENCY TRANSACTION REPORTING (CTR) - 31 CFR § 1010.311
================================================================================
MTSB files a Currency Transaction Report (FinCEN Form 112) for each deposit, withdrawal, exchange of currency, or other payment or transfer by, through, or to the Bank which involves a transaction in currency of more than $10,000 in a single business day.
- Multiple Transactions: Transactions conducted by or on behalf of the same person/entity during a single business day are aggregated. Multiple currency deposits totaling over $10,000 across multiple branches or teller windows trigger a mandatory CTR.
- Filing Timeline: CTRs must be electronically filed through the BSA E-Filing System within fifteen (15) calendar days following the date of the reportable transaction.
- Phase I and Phase II Exemptions (31 CFR § 1020.315): Certain eligible businesses (depository institutions, government entities, publicly traded corporations, qualified non-listed commercial businesses) may be exempted from CTR filing following rigorous annual review and FinCEN Form 110 designation.

SECTION 6: SANCTIONS SCREENING & OFAC COMPLIANCE (31 CFR CHAPTER V)
================================================================================
MTSB maintains automated, real-time sanctions screening filters:
1. Scope of Screening:
   - All new customer applicants, beneficial owners, and authorized signers prior to account opening.
   - All incoming and outgoing domestic and international wire transfers (Fedwire, CHIPS, SWIFT).
   - ACH transactions (Originating Depository Financial Institution [ODFI] and Receiving Depository Financial Institution [RDFI] screening).
   - Payees on official bank checks, cashier's checks, and letters of credit.
2. Watchlists Screened: OFAC Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons (SDN) list, Consolidated Non-SDN lists, Foreign Sanctions Evaders, Sectoral Sanctions Identifications (SSI), and FinCEN 311 Special Measures.
3. Blocked vs. Rejected Transactions:
   - Blocked Property: Funds or property involving target countries or individuals on the SDN list must be immediately blocked (frozen) within 24 hours. Blocked funds must be placed in a segregated, interest-bearing escrow account. A report must be filed with OFAC within ten (10) business days.
   - Rejected Transactions: Transactions involving prohibited export/import transactions where no blockable property interest exists must be rejected and reported to OFAC within ten (10) business days.

SECTION 7: RECORD RETENTION & AUDIT REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================
- Recordkeeping Mandate: Under 31 CFR § 1010.430, all records relating to CIP verification, CTR filings, SAR documentation and supporting workpapers, wire transfer logs (Travel Rule records under 31 CFR § 1010.410), and monetary instrument logs ($3,000 - $10,000 cashier's checks) must be retained for a minimum of five (5) years.
- Independent Audit: An independent third-party compliance audit of the Bank's BSA/AML/OFAC program is conducted annually, with findings delivered directly to the Board Audit Committee.
